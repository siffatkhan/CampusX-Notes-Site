11. Power Transformer

Box-Cox · Yeo-Johnson

Day 31

Power Transformer

Automatically find the best power to apply to a feature to make it close to normal.

Difference from Function Transformer

  • Function Transformer uses fixed formulas.
  • Power Transformer finds the best power automatically.

Types of Power Transformer

1. Box-Cox Transform

What it does: finds the best power \( \lambda \) for transformation.

Limitation: only works on positive values.

from sklearn.preprocessing import PowerTransformer
pt = PowerTransformer(method="box-cox")
df_transformed = pt.fit_transform(df[["col"]])

2. Yeo-Johnson Transform

What it does: same idea as Box-Cox. Supports zero and negative values.

Why better: works on all numeric data; often gives better results.

pt = PowerTransformer(method="yeo-johnson")
df_transformed = pt.fit_transform(df[["col"]])
How Power Transformer Works: it tries different powers of x, and selects the power that makes data most normal. You do not choose the power manually.

When to Use Which

Use Function Transformer when Use Power Transformer when
You understand your data well.You want automation.
You want full control.You have many features.
You know which formula fits your data.You want consistent normalization.

Test Your Understanding

Hard

5 questions on Day 31. Pick an answer for each, then check.

1. A "profit" column contains negative values (losses), zeros (break-even), and positive values (gains). Which PowerTransformer method must you use?

Explanation: Box-Cox's limitation is "only works on positive values." Yeo-Johnson "supports zero and negative values" and "works on all numeric data" — the only valid choice when the column has losses (negative) and break-even (zero) values.

2. What is the fundamental difference between Function Transformer and Power Transformer?

Explanation: "Function Transformer uses fixed formulas. Power Transformer finds the best power automatically." You don't manually pick the power for PowerTransformer — it tries different powers and selects the one that makes data most normal.

3. You have deep domain knowledge that a specific column follows a known right-skew pattern best fixed by a square-root relationship, and you want full manual control over the exact formula. Per the "When to Use Which" guidance, what should you reach for?

Explanation: "Use Function Transformer when: You understand your data well. You want full control. You know which formula fits your data." That's exactly this scenario — known formula + desire for manual control.

4. You have 40 numeric features across a large dataset and want each one automatically normalized without manually inspecting and choosing a formula for each. Which approach fits best?

Explanation: "Use Power Transformer when: You want automation. You have many features. You want consistent normalization." That is exactly this scenario — 40 features, no manual per-column formula selection desired.

5. You run PowerTransformer(method="box-cox").fit_transform(df[["price"]]) and it throws an error. The "price" column contains a few rows equal to 0. Why?

Explanation: Box-Cox's stated limitation is that it "only works on positive values" — zero fails that requirement. Switching to method="yeo-johnson" would fix this, since it "supports zero and negative values."
