Day 33

Handling Mixed Data

What is Mixed Data

One column contains both letters and numbers.

Example — Cabin column: B5, D1, A8

Problem

Model cannot learn from mixed types directly.

Solution

Split into two columns: Cabin_Letter (B, D, A) and Cabin_Number (5, 1, 8). Then: encode letters, keep numbers as numeric.

Handling Date and Time Variables

Expense Tracker ML Example

Raw data: Amount, Date, Time, Category

Feature engineering:

From Date
  • Day of week
  • Month
  • Is weekend
From Time
  • Hour
  • Morning, afternoon, evening, night
From Amount
  • Binned spending level: Low, Medium, High
  • Binary: High expense or not

Use cases: predict next expense category, detect unusual spending, forecast monthly spending.

Test Your Understanding

Hard

5 questions on Day 33–34. Pick an answer for each, then check.

1. A "Cabin" column has values like "B5", "D1", "A8". Why can't a model use this column directly?

Explanation: "Mixed data" means "one column contains both letters and numbers." The stated problem is: "Model cannot learn from mixed types directly." The fix is splitting into Cabin_Letter and Cabin_Number, not imputation, scaling, or cardinality reduction.

2. After splitting "Cabin" into Cabin_Letter and Cabin_Number, what should happen to each new column?

Explanation: Per the notes: "Then: Encode letters. Keep numbers as numeric." Cabin_Letter (B, D, A) is categorical and needs encoding (e.g. one-hot); Cabin_Number (5, 1, 8) is already numeric and is left as-is.

3. From a timestamp "2026-01-28 14:45" (a Wednesday), which extracted feature would correctly read "No"?

Explanation: Per the worked example for 2026-01-28 14:45: "Day of week: Wednesday" and "Is weekend: No." Wednesday is a weekday, so the boolean "Is weekend" feature is "No" — matching the source example exactly.

4. In the Expense Tracker ML example, which engineered feature is derived from the "Amount" field, not from Date or Time?

Explanation: Per the notes: "From Amount: Binned spending level: Low, Medium, High. Binary: High expense or not." "Is weekend" and "Month" come From Date, and "Morning, afternoon, evening, night" comes From Time — Amount is the only source of the binned spending level feature.

5. Both Day 33 (mixed data) and the binning/encoding concepts from earlier sections converge in the Expense Tracker example. What connects "Binned spending level" in Day 34 back to the earlier binning material?

Explanation: Binning ("Discretization converts continuous numerical data into discrete categories") is a general technique applicable to any continuous feature — here it's reused on Amount to create "Binned spending level: Low, Medium, High," the same Low/Medium/High pattern used in the general binning examples, not something specific to dates or mixed columns.
